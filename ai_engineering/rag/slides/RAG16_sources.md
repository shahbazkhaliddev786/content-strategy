# RAG #16 (format v3): sources (not posted)

Every example output on the slides is real. `RAG16_verify.py` re-runs each one and checks the slide text against it (18 checks).

- **Library:** `langchain-experimental` 0.4.2, `SemanticChunker` (read from the installed source): sentences split with `(?<=[.?!])\s+`; each sentence combined with `buffer_size=1` neighbour on each side; every combined group embedded; cosine distance between consecutive groups; cut where the distance is strictly above the threshold.
- **Threshold formulas** (`_calculate_breakpoint_threshold`): percentile = `np.percentile(distances, 95)`; standard_deviation = mean + 3 × std; interquartile = mean + 1.5 × IQR (not Q3 + 1.5 × IQR); gradient = 95th percentile of `np.gradient(distances)`. Defaults from `BREAKPOINT_DEFAULTS`.
- **Size:** the constructor has `min_chunk_size` and no maximum.
- **Embeddings in the examples:** spaCy 3.8 `en_core_web_md` 3.8.0 (300-d word vectors; a sentence vector is the average), downloaded from the spaCy models release on GitHub. Hugging Face models can't be downloaded from the build environment. The slide names the model.
- **Measurements:** 8-sentence refund text: distances 0.019, 0.023, 0.016, 0.013, 0.033, 0.025, 0.010; 95th percentile 0.0308; mean + 3 × std 0.0419. 24-sentence refund text (1,227 characters): `standard_deviation` returns one chunk; a 500-character `RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter` (sentence separator, `keep_separator="end"`) cuts it into 465, 479 and 281 characters. Finding the cuts embedded 24 inputs totalling 3,551 characters (2.9×). With random distances, 20, 40 and 100 gaps give 1, 2 and 5 cuts above the 95th percentile.
- **Deprecation:** importing `langchain_experimental` 0.4.2 raises: "`langchain-experimental` is being sunset and is no longer actively maintained. See https://github.com/langchain-ai/langchain-experimental/issues/87 for details."
- **LlamaIndex:** `SemanticSplitterNodeParser` defaults `buffer_size=1`, `breakpoint_percentile_threshold=95`, and cuts where the distance is above the percentile: https://docs.llamaindex.ai/en/latest/api_reference/node_parsers/semantic_splitter/
- **Study:** Renyi Qu, Ruixuan Tu, Forrest Bao (Vectara; University of Wisconsin–Madison), "Is Semantic Chunking Worth the Computational Cost?", arXiv 2410.13070, Oct 2024: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13070. Abstract: tested on document retrieval, evidence retrieval and retrieval-based answer generation; "the computational costs associated with semantic chunking are not justified by consistent performance gains". Introduction: advantages are "highly task-dependent".
